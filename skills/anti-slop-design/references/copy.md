# Copy

Words are design. Generated copy has its own tells.

## Write like a person who knows the subject

- **Say what it does, to whom.** "Checks your public URLs every minute and writes a status page." Not "Supercharge your uptime."
- **Use plain verbs and nouns.** Concrete beats abstract: "bookings", "invoices", "slipway".
- **State limits.** A "what it does not do" section is credible and rare.
- **Use the reader's words.** If customers say "rota", do not write "scheduling solution".

## Words to be suspicious of

seamless, elevate, unlock, supercharge, revolutionise, empower, cutting-edge, next-generation, game-changer, leverage, all-in-one, streamline, harness, delve, tapestry, "in today's fast-paced world", "take it to the next level". If one appears, ask what the plain version is.

## Shapes to avoid

- The triplet: three adjectives or three benefits with identical grammar, every time.
- "It is not just X, it is Y."
- Headlines that are a verb plus "your" plus an abstract noun ("Transform your workflow").
- Several long dashes in one paragraph. Use a full stop or a colon.
- A question as a heading, answered by the next sentence.

## Buttons and links

Name the action and its result: "Download the checklist", "Book a 20 minute call", "See the three checks". Not "Get started" or "Learn more".

## Numbers and names

Use only figures you can source, and cite them. Use real names with permission or none. See `content-integrity.md`.
