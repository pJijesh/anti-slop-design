# The brief

Write this before any markup. Six lines is enough. If a line is hard to write, the page will be vague in that place.

1. **Job.** What is the page for, in one sentence with a verb? "Lets a parent check whether swimming lessons have space."
2. **Visitor.** Who arrives, from where, in what state of mind, on what device? "A tired parent on a phone at 22:00."
3. **Next step.** The single thing you want them to do. One. If there are two, one is secondary and looks it.
4. **Feel.** Three adjectives, then one thing it is the opposite of. "Calm, exact, municipal. Not a startup."
5. **Not.** Two things the page must not become. Often the most useful line. "Not a brochure. Not a dashboard."
6. **Real content.** Every real sentence, number, name, image and link you actually have. Mark what is missing.

## Why this matters

A model asked for "a landing page for X" fills the gaps with the average landing page. The brief closes the gaps with decisions. The average landing page is what readers call AI-generated.

## Missing content

If the real content is not there, you have three honest options: ask for it, write the page around what you do have, or leave a clearly marked gap such as `[Add the opening hours]`. The fourth option, inventing it, is never acceptable.
