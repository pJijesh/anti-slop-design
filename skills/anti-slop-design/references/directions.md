# Directions

A direction is a set of linked choices, not a theme. Choose one that suits the brief, or invent your own using the same checklist. Do not blend three. Fix the choices in writing before you build.

For each direction: what it suits, the type, the colour logic, how it divides space, and what to watch for. Typefaces are suggestions with safe fallbacks; use what the project already loads, and never assume a web font is available.

## 1. Printed document

Suits: documentation, reports, public information, tools for professionals.
Type: a book serif for text (Georgia, Iowan Old Style, Source Serif) with a monospace for labels and data.
Colour: warm paper, near-black ink, one signal colour used only for the thing that needs action.
Space: a single narrow column, hairline rules for sections, tables for data.
Watch for: it can turn dull. Give the headline real scale and weight.

## 2. Instrument panel

Suits: operations tools, status pages, technical products.
Type: a monospace or a narrow grotesque in small, tight sizes; numerals in tabular figures.
Colour: dark neutral or off-white, one state colour per meaning (up, down, warning) and no more.
Space: dense grid with consistent gutters; every number aligned.
Watch for: density that ignores phone use; keep tap targets at least 44 pixels.

## 3. Trade catalogue

Suits: shops, makers, services with physical products.
Type: a sturdy serif for headings and a plain sans for details, set like a parts list.
Colour: taken from the product's materials (oak, slate, enamel), flat, no gradients.
Space: ruled rows, product first, prices and details in a column that lines up.
Watch for: imagery. Use real photographs or none.

## 4. Poster

Suits: events, campaigns, announcements with one message.
Type: one very large display face, one small text face.
Colour: two colours plus paper, high contrast, and a deliberate clash allowed.
Space: asymmetric, one dominant element, large blank areas that are meant.
Watch for: legibility of the small text, and that the date, place and action are findable in three seconds.

## 5. Field notes

Suits: personal sites, portfolios, writing, small studios.
Type: a humanist sans or a sturdy serif, with generous line height and a visible baseline rhythm.
Colour: muted, from one real photograph or object; accent used on links only.
Space: left-aligned text, wide margins, small captions set in the margin.
Watch for: sameness. Pick one odd, specific detail, such as a custom list marker or a distinctive date format, and use it consistently.

## 6. Municipal clarity

Suits: public services, forms, health and finance information, anything where trust and ease matter most.
Type: a plain, highly legible sans at 18 pixels or more, strong headings, no decoration.
Colour: high contrast, a single blue or green action colour, errors in a distinct colour with text, not colour alone.
Space: one task per screen, large controls, clear error messages in plain words.
Watch for: blandness is the point here; make it clear, not clever.

## Inventing your own

Answer these, in order, and write the answers down: What material or tradition does the subject come from? What would its printed matter look like? What is the one colour it owns? What typeface would its signwriter use? What does it do that its competitors do not?
