# Content integrity

A page that looks real and contains made-up facts is worse than an ugly honest one. This is the floor, not a style choice.

## Never invent

- Statistics ("10,000+ users", "99.9% uptime", "4.9 stars").
- Customer names, logos, case studies, awards, press mentions.
- Testimonials, quotes, reviews, and the people who supposedly gave them.
- Team members, job titles, credentials, certifications.
- Prices, dates, availability, legal text.
- Photographs presented as real people or places when they are stock or generated.

## What to do instead

- **Use what you have.** Write the page around real, supplied material.
- **Leave a marked gap.** `[Add the opening hours]` is honest. A made-up number is not.
- **Show how it works instead of claiming that it works.** A worked example, a real screenshot, a sample output.
- **Source it.** If you use a figure, say where it comes from and when, in the page.
- **Say it is an example.** If the page is a demonstration, label it so in the page, as the example here does.

## Imagery

Use only images you have the right to use, and credit them where required. Prefer a real photograph, a diagram that explains something, or nothing. Do not use generated faces as people.

## In the linter

`invented-claims`, `placeholder-text`, `stock-names` and `testimonials` look for the common signs. They can only see patterns, not truth. You are responsible for the rest.
